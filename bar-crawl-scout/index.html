What the numbers mean (the formula, owned)

R26 / R27 = value rating each season. tier from ADP (top-12 = 100, then 85, 68, 48, 32, 20) times a stage curve, plus an elite bonus for the genuine top dogs (ADP 1-4 get +12, 5-8 +7) because the single best player wins you weeks. A young riser gains into 2027; an aging vet decays. The key change: a final-year keeper's R27 is not 0, it is a replacement value (52). Keeping Chase this year does not cost you 2027, you simply keep someone else next year, so a one-year superstar is rewarded, not punished to zero. He carries his elite 2026 plus a free roster slot in 2027.

P27 still shows "-" for a final-year player because that is his own production (gone in 2027), separate from the slot value baked into R27.

P26 / P27 = projected half-PPR season points (6-point passing TDs). Production, to sanity-check value.

WIN = the committed formula: WIN = R26 x w1 + R27 x w2. Win-now w = (1.0, 0.15); Balanced (1.0, 1.0); Future (0.4, 1.0). A final-year elite like Chase scores roughly 112 (R26 with elite bonus) plus 52 (replacement R27), so he sits near the very top in Win-now and stays high in Balanced rather than collapsing. A genuine two-year phenom (Jeanty, ~85 + ~102) still edges him in Balanced, which is correct, but in Win-now the best-player-alive wins. Different players top each mode, by design.

Trade builder

Pick players and picks from the dropdowns. This does not add 50 + 50 = 100. Depth is discounted because you start a fixed lineup, and the single best asset carries a scarcity premium, so consolidating up into a stud beats hoarding mediums. Picks are valued as the real player you would land after 30 keepers are off the board, so a 1st here is weaker than a redraft or dynasty 1st, and an elite player outvalues a pick, especially in Win-now.
